Task 7: Electric field of a uniformly charged sphere

A spherical volume charge has density ρ0 for 0 ≤ r ≤ a and zero outside. Its centre is the origin. Find the field everywhere in vacuum, where D = ε0E.

Connected topic: Gauss’s law (§1.10) turns spherical symmetry into a flux equation on a concentric sphere.

7: Field inside and outside

Try first: choose a concentric spherical Gaussian surface of radius r, and find its enclosed charge separately for r ≤ a and r > a.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

7 · Gauss’s law in the two regions

  1. Spherical symmetry makes the field radial and equally strong everywhere on a concentric Gaussian sphere. Its area is 4πr2, so the flux law reduces to:

    ∯ D · dA = Dr(r) 4πr2 = Qenc(r)

    Since D = ε0E, solving this gives Er once we know the enclosed charge.

  2. For 0 < r ≤ a, the Gaussian sphere contains only a smaller volume of the uniform density. The spherical volume is 4πr3/3:

    Qenc(r) = ρ04πr33

  3. Substitute that enclosed charge into Gauss’s law and divide by 4πε0r2:

    Er(r) = Qenc4πε0r2 = ρ0r3ε0,   0 ≤ r ≤ a

  4. For r > a, the Gaussian sphere encloses all the charge, whose volume is the full sphere of radius a:

    Qtotal = ρ04πa33;   Er(r) = ρ0a33ε0r2,   r > a

  5. Result: for the positive density shown, the vector field points radially outward. Inside:

    E(r) = ρ0r3ε0er,   0 ≤ r ≤ a

    Outside, all charge is enclosed:

    E(r) = ρ0a33ε0r2er,   r > a

    At the origin, E = 0; negative ρ0 reverses the radial direction.

    Check: both formulas give E(a) = ρ0a/(3ε0), so the field is continuous at the surface. Far away it falls as 1/r2, as if the total charge were concentrated at the centre. With SI inputs, E is in N/C.

Task 8: Parallel plates with a dielectric cylinder

Two circular metal plates have radius a2 and separation h. A concentric dielectric cylinder of radius a1 fills the gap between the plates; the annular region from a1 to a2 is air. The source assumes uniform axial fields in both regions and neglects fringing at the plate edge.

Connected topics: Capacitance (§1.13) relates charge and voltage; field energy (§1.14) gives the stored energy. Interpret ε1 as the absolute permittivity of the cylinder, as distinguished from ε0 for air in the figure.

The source labels the applied voltage U10 with an arrow from the upper plate to the lower one. Use the standard convention U10 = V1 − V0, upper minus lower; for positive U10, the field points down, along −ez.

8a: Field between the plates

Try first: relate the potential difference to the axial field, then use tangential-field continuity at the cylindrical material boundary.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8a · One voltage, two regions

  1. The field is uniform and axial in each region. Moving from the lower plate at z = 0 to the upper plate at z = h gives the upper-minus-lower potential:

    U10 = V1 − V0 = −∫0h Ez dz

  2. The radial boundary at ρ = a1 is parallel to the z-directed field. Its tangential field component is continuous, so the assumed axial components agree:

    E1 = E2 = Ez

  3. Because each field is uniform over the gap, the integral is Ezh. Solve for the common component:

    E1 = E2 = −U10h

  4. Result: in the requested gap (not inside the metal), the field is the same everywhere:

    E = −U10h ez,   0 ≤ ρ ≤ a2,   0 < z < h

    The same field applies on both sides of ρ = a1 under the uniform-field approximation. The material changes D, not this tangential E.

    Check: E has units V/m. For positive upper-minus-lower voltage, potential decreases in the +z direction, so E points downward.

8b: Charge on the underside of the upper plate

Try first: find the free surface charge density in each patch from D = εE and the normal pointing from the upper conductor into the gap.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8b · Add the two material patches

  1. The outward normal from the lower face of the upper metal plate into the gap is −ez. The conductor boundary relation gives free surface charge density σf = D · n. Since E = −(U10/h)ez, each patch has positive σ for positive U10.

  2. Over the dielectric circle, D = ε1E; its area is πa12:

    Qcore = σ1πa12 = ε1U10hπa12

  3. Over the air annulus, D = ε0E; its area is the whole plate disk minus the core disk:

    Qair = σ2π(a22 − a12) = ε0U10hπ(a22 − a12)

  4. Add the two disjoint patches on the same metal face:

    Q = Qcore + Qair = πU10h[ε1a12 + ε0(a22 − a12)]

    Result: this is the charge on the underside of the upper plate. For positive U10 it is positive; the lower plate carries the opposite charge.

    Check: each term has permittivity × voltage × area / length, which is coulombs. If a1 = 0 the full plate is air; if a1 = a2 it is fully filled by the dielectric.

8c: Capacitance

Try first: divide the upper-plate charge by the applied voltage.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8c · Charge-to-voltage ratio

  1. Capacitance is defined by C = Q/U. Divide the expression from 8b by U10:

    C = πh[ε1a12 + ε0(a22 − a12)]

  2. Result: this is the capacitance under the stated uniform-field model. The two regions behave like capacitors in parallel because they span the same voltage and their charges add.

    Check: it equals the sum of area/permittivity contributions divided by h; capacitance has units farads.

8d: Energy change when the dielectric is removed

Try first: compute the initial and final capacitances, then compare their field energies at the same fixed voltage.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8d · Fixed-voltage energy change

  1. Initially the capacitance is the value from 8c. After removal, the whole plate area is air:

    Ci = πh[ε1a12 + ε0(a22 − a12)];   Cf = ε0πa22h

  2. With voltage held fixed, stored field energy is W = ½C U2. Subtract the initial energy from the final energy:

    ΔWfield = ½(Cf − Ci)U102

  3. The air-region terms cancel, leaving the contribution from the removed core:

    ΔWfield = −πa12(ε1 − ε0)2hU102

  4. Follow the energy that leaves. With U10 fixed, the plate charge changes by ΔQ = (Cf − Ci)U10 < 0, so charge flows back into the source. The energy the source delivers is Wsource = U10ΔQ = 2ΔWfield, a negative number: the source takes energy back. Whoever pulls the core out supplies the difference:

    Wmech = ΔWfield − Wsource = −ΔWfield = πa12(ε1 − ε0)2hU102

  5. Result: if ε1 > ε0, the stored field energy decreases by this amount (ΔWfield < 0), measured in joules. Positive mechanical work of the same size is needed to pull the core out: at fixed voltage the field draws the dielectric into the gap, and the source takes back twice the field-energy drop.

    Check: if the core is indistinguishable from air, ε1 = ε0, the change is zero. The magnitude scales with the removed area and with voltage squared.
