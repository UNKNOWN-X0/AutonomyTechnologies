Task 4: Cylinder coordinates

The space charge density is constant, ϱV = ϱ0, in the hollow cylindrical region a ≤ ρ ≤ b, 0 ≤ φ ≤ 2π, 0 ≤ z ≤ c, and zero outside. Assume 0 ≤ a < b and c > 0.

Connected topic: Flux and Gauss’s law (§1.10) develops cylindrical coordinates and radial geometry; the volume element supplies the appropriate shell weighting here.

4: Total charge in the hollow cylinder

Try first: write the cylindrical volume element, then integrate across the inner and outer radii, the full angle, and the height.

4 · Cylindrical volume integral

  1. Total charge is the volume integral of volume charge density over the charged region. In cylindrical coordinates, a small volume is a wedge-shaped piece with volume ρ dρ dφ dz:

    Q = ∭V ϱV dV,   dV = ρ dρ dφ dz

  2. Substitute the constant density and the stated limits. The inner radius a excludes the empty central cylinder:

    Q = ϱ0 ∫0c ∫02π ∫ab ρ dρ dφ dz

    Q = ϱ0 ∫0c ∫02π [(ρ2/2)]ab dφ dz

  3. Evaluate the radial term, then the full revolution and cylinder height:

    Q = ϱ0 ((b2 − a2)/2)(2π)c

    Q = ϱ0 π(b2 − a2)c

    The sign follows the sign of ϱ0. Check: the factor π(b² − a²)c is exactly the hollow cylinder’s volume, and ϱ0 times volume has units C.

Task 5: Spherical coordinates

The density varies radially as ϱV(r) = ϱ0r2/a2 inside a sphere of radius a and is zero for r > a. Assume a > 0.

Connected topic: Flux and Gauss’s law (§1.10) uses spherical symmetry and spherical shells; here each shell contributes its density times its shell volume.

5: Total charge in the sphere

Try first: multiply the radial density by the spherical shell volume 4πr²dr and integrate from the centre to a.

5 · Spherical volume integral

  1. For a spherically symmetric density, integrate over concentric shells. A shell at radius r has volume 4πr²dr, and only radii 0 through a contain charge:

    Q = ∭V ϱV dV = 4π ∫0a ϱV(r) r2 dr

  2. Insert the given density. One factor r² comes from the shell area and another from ϱV:

    Q = 4π ∫0a (ϱ0r2/a2) r2 dr

    Q = (4πϱ0/a2) ∫0a r4 dr

  3. Integrate r4 and apply both limits:

    Q = (4πϱ0/a2)[r5/5]0a

    Q = 4πϱ0a3/5

    The sign follows ϱ0. Check: the average density is 3ϱ0/5, giving (3ϱ0/5)(4πa³/3), the same result.
