Task 8: Electrostatics

Two square plates of side a are separated by total distance d. Initially, dielectric 1 (εr,1) fills the upper half and dielectric 2 (εr,2) fills the lower half, each of thickness d/2. Edge effects are neglected; the exposed surrounding region is air with relative permittivity 1. The voltage arrow points downward; take positive U0 to produce a downward field. Both relative permittivities are much greater than 1.

Connected topic: Capacitance (§1.13) relates plate geometry and permittivity to charge and voltage; the interface conditions explain how the displacement crosses the two layers.

8a: Find D and E

Try first: use continuity of the normal displacement at the dielectric boundary and add the two half-gap voltage drops.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8a · Fields in the two layers

  1. With no free charge at the internal interface, the normal component of D is the same in both layers. The voltage arrow sets the field downward for positive U0; denote the downward displacement magnitude by D0:

    D1 = D2 = −D0ez

  2. In each linear dielectric, D = ε0εrE. The downward potential drop across each thickness d/2 adds to U0:

    U0 = (|D|/(ε0εr,1))(d/2) + (|D|/(ε0εr,2))(d/2)

    |D| = 2ε0εr,1εr,2U0 / [d(εr,1 + εr,2)]

  3. Apply the constitutive relation separately to each layer:

    D = −[2ε0εr,1εr,2U0/(d(εr,1+εr,2))]ez

    E1 = −[2εr,2U0/(d(εr,1+εr,2))]ez,   E2 = −[2εr,1U0/(d(εr,1+εr,2))]ez

    For negative U0, the vectors reverse. Check: substituting the two field magnitudes into the half-gap drops gives exactly U0.

8b: Equivalent circuit before the shift

Try first: decide whether the layers lie one after another along the field or side by side across the plate area.

8b · Series connection

  1. Every field line crosses dielectric 1 and then dielectric 2. The same free charge magnitude sits on each plate, while the voltage divides between the layers, so the capacitors are in series:

    C1 — C2

  2. The interface has no free surface charge, so the normal displacement (and therefore the series charge) is common to both. Check: a parallel model would incorrectly assign the full plate voltage to each half-thickness layer.

8c: Total capacitance before the shift

Try first: calculate each layer’s plate capacitance using area a2 and thickness d/2, then combine in series.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8c · Series capacitance

  1. For a parallel-plate layer, C = εA/t. Each layer has area a2 and thickness d/2:

    C1 = 2ε0εr,1a2/d,   C2 = 2ε0εr,2a2/d

  2. Series capacitances add by reciprocals:

    1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2

    C = 2ε0a2εr,1εr,2 / [d(εr,1+εr,2)]

    Check: this is smaller than either layer capacitance, as a series equivalent must be.

8d: Surface charge on the lower face of the top plate

Try first: dot D with the outward normal from the metal into the dielectric at that face.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8d · Free surface charge density

  1. The bottom face of the top plate points outward from the conductor in the −ez direction. The free charge density on a conductor surface is D · n just outside the metal:

    σ0 = D · (−ez)

  2. Since D also points downward for positive U0, the dot product is positive:

    σ0 = 2ε0εr,1εr,2U0 / [d(εr,1+εr,2)]

    The units are C/m². Reversing the applied voltage reverses the surface-charge sign.

The lower dielectric is now shifted out along +ex by a distance x, with 0 ≤ x ≤ a. Its overlap width is a−x; the exposed gap beneath the upper dielectric is filled with air.

8e: Equivalent circuit after the shift

Try first: identify the full-area layer crossed by every field line, then split the lower half into the two side-by-side regions.

8e · Series and parallel sections

  1. All paths pass through dielectric 1 across the full area a2. In the lower half, some area a(a−x) contains dielectric 2 and the remaining area a x contains air. Those lower branches share the same voltage:

    C1 — (C2 ∥ CL)

  2. Thus C2 and CL are in parallel with one another, and their equivalent is in series with C1. Check: at x = 0 the air branch has zero area; at x = a the lower dielectric branch has zero area.

8f: Capacitance as a function of x

Try first: find each lower-region capacitance from its area and thickness, add the parallel branches, then combine that result in series with C1.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8f · Capacitance after displacement

  1. The upper layer keeps area a2 and thickness d/2. The two lower branches each have thickness d/2:

    C1 = 2ε0εr,1a2/d

    C2(x) = 2ε0εr,2a(a−x)/d,   CL(x) = 2ε0a x/d

  2. Add the parallel pair and then use the series formula:

    Cp(x) = C2(x) + CL(x) = (2ε0a/d)[εr,2a + (1−εr,2)x]

    C(x) = C1Cp(x)C1+Cp(x)

    C(x) = (2ε0a/d) εr,1a[εr,2a+(1−εr,2)x]εr,1a+εr,2a+(1−εr,2)x

    Endpoint checks: C(0) matches 8c, while C(a) is the series combination of the upper dielectric and an air layer of thickness d/2.

8g: Minimum and maximum capacitance

Try first: check how the lower parallel capacitance changes with x, then use that the series equivalent increases when either component capacitance increases.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

8g · Locate the extrema

  1. The lower equivalent is linear in x:

    Cp(x) = (2ε0a/d)[εr,2a + (1−εr,2)x]

    Because εr,2 is much greater than 1, its slope is negative. The series equivalent C1Cp/(C1+Cp) increases monotonically with Cp.

  2. Therefore the total capacitance decreases throughout 0 ≤ x ≤ a:

    Maximum at x = 0; minimum at x = a.

    If εr,2 were exactly 1, the lower region would be air everywhere and the capacitance would be constant; the sheet specifies εr,2 much greater than 1, excluding that degenerate case.
