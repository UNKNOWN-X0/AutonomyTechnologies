Task 6: Line and point charges

Use the shown xy cross-section: Q1 = (−a, 0), Q2 = (a, 0), the line λ1 passes through (0, a), and λ2 passes through (0, −a/2). Both lines run parallel to z. Take air as vacuum for the field formulas, as instructed, and assume a > 0.

Connected topic: Electric field strength (§1.3) gives Coulomb’s field and vector superposition. The line-charge formula is applied with its radial direction converted to Cartesian components.

6a: Field of Q1 at Q2

Try first: find the displacement from Q1 to Q2, then use Coulomb’s field with that direction and distance.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

6a · Point-charge field

  1. The source is at x = −a and the field point at x = a, so the displacement points along +x and has length 2a:

    R21 = 2a ex,   R21 = 2a

  2. Coulomb’s field is the source charge times the radial unit vector divided by 4πε0R²:

    EQ1(Q2) = (Q1/(4πε0R212)) ex

    EQ1(Q2) = Q1/(16πε0a2) ex

    The sign of Q1 sets whether the field points +x or −x. The 1/(2a)² factor is easy to miss.

6b: Force of Q1 on Q2

Try first: multiply the field from 6a by the charge that experiences it, Q2.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

6b · Point-charge force

  1. The electric force on a charge is its charge times the electric field at its position:

    F12 = Q2 EQ1(Q2)

  2. Substitute the result from 6a:

    F12 = Q1Q2/(16πε0a2) ex

    Like signs give a force toward +x on Q2; opposite signs reverse it. Its units are newtons because charge times field is C·N/C.

6c: Field of λ1 and λ2 at Q2

Try first: form the transverse displacement from each line to Q2, normalize it, and add the two line fields.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

6c · Infinite-line fields

  1. For λ1, the displacement from its axis (0, a) to Q2 (a, 0) is (a, −a). Its radius is √2 a and its radial unit vector is (ex − ey)/√2:

    Eλ1 = (λ1/(2πε0√2 a))((ex − ey)/√2) = λ1/(4πε0a)(ex − ey)

  2. For λ2, the displacement is (a, a/2), with radius √5 a/2 and unit direction (2ex + ey)/√5:

    Eλ2 = (λ2/(πε0a√5))((2ex + ey)/√5) = λ2/(5πε0a)(2ex + ey)

  3. Add the x and y components separately:

    Elines = (1/(πε0a))[(λ1/4 + 2λ2/5)ex + (−λ1/4 + λ2/5)ey]

    Check the second line’s distance is a√5/2, not a√5; its source is only a/2 below the x-axis. Each field component has units N/C.

6d: Force of the lines on Q2

Try first: use the field sum from 6c and multiply by Q2.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

6d · Force from both lines

  1. Both line fields act at Q2, so their combined force is Q2 times their vector sum:

    Flines,2 = Q2 Elines

  2. Therefore:

    Flines,2 = Q2/(πε0a)[(λ1/4 + 2λ2/5)ex + (−λ1/4 + λ2/5)ey]

    A negative component indicates the corresponding force points along the negative axis; the force components are in newtons.

6e: Total force on Q2

Try first: add the point-charge force from 6b to the line-charge force from 6d, keeping x and y components separate.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

6e · Superposition of all forces

  1. Electrostatic forces add linearly, so the total is the point-charge contribution plus the two-line contribution:

    F2 = F12 + Flines,2

  2. Collect the coefficients of each Cartesian direction:

    F2 = [Q1Q2/(16πε0a2) + Q2(λ1/4 + 2λ2/5)/(πε0a)]ex + [Q2(−λ1/4 + λ2/5)/(πε0a)]ey

    Check: if both line densities vanish, this reduces to the Coulomb force in 6b.

6f: Condition for zero total force

Try first: set both components from 6e to zero and solve the y equation before the x equation.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

6f · Balance the two components

  1. Q2 is nonzero, so the y-component vanishes when:

    −λ1/4 + λ2/5 = 0   ⇒   λ2 = 5λ1/4

  2. Insert this relation into the x-component, including the field from Q1:

    Q1/(16a2) + λ1/(4a) + 2(5λ1/4)/(5a) = 0

    Q1/(16a2) + 3λ1/(4a) = 0   ⇒   Q1 = −12aλ1

  3. Thus the required condition is λ2 = 5λ1/4 and Q1 = −12aλ1 (equivalently λ1 = −Q1/(12a), λ2 = −5Q1/(48a)). These signs make the line forces cancel in y and balance Q1’s x contribution.

Task 7: Point charge

Take Q > 0 as the reference scale. The figure places Q1 = √5 Q at (0, a) and Q2 = √2 Q at (3a, 0). The supplied net forces on Q1 and Q2 are F1 = F0[−(3 + 5√5)ex + ey] and F2 = F0(13ex − 11ey), with F0 = Q²/(40πε0a²). The charges are in vacuum and a > 0; F0 is a force scale measured in newtons.

Connected topic: Electric field strength (§1.3) connects Coulomb force, displacement direction and vector superposition.

7a: Force F12 of Q2 on Q1

Try first: find the vector from Q2 to Q1 and substitute both charges and their separation into Coulomb’s law.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

7a · First known pair force

  1. The displacement from Q2 at (3a, 0) to Q1 at (0, a) is (−3a, a), and the distance is √10 a:

    R12 = a(−3ex + ey),   R12 = √10 a

  2. Coulomb force on Q1 from Q2 is kQ1Q2 times the unit displacement divided by R². Here Q1Q2 = √10 Q²:

    F12 = (1/(4πε0)) (√10 Q2/(10√10 a2))(−3ex + ey)

    F12 = F0(−3ex + ey)

    The displacement points from the source toward Q1, so this force points down-left for like charges.

7b: Force F21 of Q1 on Q2

Try first: use Newton’s third law for the same pair.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

7b · Equal and opposite force

  1. Forces between a pair have equal magnitude and opposite direction:

    F21 = −F12

  2. Therefore:

    F21 = F0(3ex − ey)

    The two pair forces sum to zero when applied to both charges together.

7c: Force F13 of Q3 on Q1

Try first: subtract the known Q2 contribution from the supplied net force on Q1.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

7c · Isolate the remaining force

  1. The given resultant on Q1 is F1 = F12 + F13, so rearrange to isolate the force from Q3:

    F13 = F1 − F12

  2. Subtract the components (the +ey terms cancel):

    F13 = F0[−(3 + 5√5)ex + ey − (−3ex + ey)]

    F13 = −5√5 F0ex

    Its purely horizontal direction places Q3 on the horizontal line through Q1.

7d: Force F23 of Q3 on Q2

Try first: add F12 to the net force on Q2, using F21 = −F12.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

7d · Second remaining pair force

  1. The given resultant on Q2 is F2 = F21 + F23. Since F21 = −F12:

    F23 = F2 + F12

  2. Combine components:

    F23 = F0[(13 − 3)ex + (−11 + 1)ey]

    F23 = 10F0(ex − ey)

    Its direction line is diagonal, with equal x and negative y components.

7e: Coordinates of Q3

Try first: intersect the horizontal line through Q1 with the diagonal direction line through Q2.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

7e · Locate the third charge

  1. F13 has no y component, so the line joining Q1 and Q3 is horizontal. Since y1 = a, this gives y3 = a.

  2. F23 is parallel to (1, −1). The line through Q2 = (3a, 0) in that direction has form (3a, 0) + t(1, −1). Its y-coordinate equals a when t = −a:

    (x3, y3) = (3a, 0) − a(1, −1) = (2a, a)

  3. Result: Q3 is at (2a, a). This position also gives Q1Q3 = 2a and Q2Q3 = √2 a.

7f: Charge ratio Q3/Q

Try first: use the magnitude of F13, the separation 2a, and Q1 = √5 Q.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

7f · Find the third charge

  1. The force magnitude between Q1 and Q3 is 5√5 F0. Coulomb’s law at separation 2a gives:

    5√5 F0 = (1/(4πε0)) (√5 Q |Q3|/(2a)2)

  2. Insert F0 = Q²/(40πε0a²) and cancel common positive factors:

    5√5 Q2/(40πε0a2) = √5 Q|Q3|/(16πε0a2)

    |Q3| = 2Q

  3. F13 points from Q3 toward Q1, and Q3 is to the right of Q1; the force is repulsive. Thus Q3 has the same sign as Q1, and Q3/Q = 2.

7g: Total force F3 on Q3

Try first: reverse F13 and F23 by Newton’s third law, then add them.

Attempted it? Open the worked steps

7g · Resultant on Q3

  1. The force on Q3 from Q1 is opposite the force of Q3 on Q1; the same reversal holds for the Q2 pair:

    F31 = −F13 = 5√5 F0ex,   F32 = −F23 = −10F0ex + 10F0ey

  2. Now add the two forces acting on Q3:

    F3 = F0[(5√5 − 10)ex + 10ey]

    Its positive y component and small positive x component agree with Q3 lying above and to the right of Q2. Numerically, 5√5 − 10 ≈ 1.18.
