Version Log

From Fundamentals to AI Apocalypse · newest first

What changed on the site and when. Chapters are added as the semester's lectures arrive; each version groups the changes released together.

2.43 Fractions stacked everywhere

11 November 2026

  • About 820 quotients written with a slash, such as ρ0a/(3ε0) or (y − 3)/2, are now stacked fractions, numerator over denominator, in every guide, exercise, tutorial and the appendix. In sentences they are set a little smaller than the text, so a line opens only slightly; in formula lines they keep full size. The PDFs match.
  • A slash is kept only inside exponents and limits, in units such as m/s, and between words. The page checker now flags any quotient still written with a slash.

2.42 The side bar as an instrument

11 November 2026

  • A readout at the top of the contents names the section you are reading in large figures, counts it (“6 of 22”) and fills a strip of one segment per entry as you go; its title settles letter by letter when you move on.
  • Every entry is a station on the route line: hollow ahead, solid once passed. A reticle of corner brackets glides to the section being read and locks onto it, and each group heading counts how far in you are (“5/14”).
  • In the Chapters list each subject is named by its colour square. On a phone, the same progress strip runs along the bottom of the Contents bar. Reduced motion keeps all of it, without the movement.

2.41 A shorter Chapters list

11 November 2026

  • The Chapters list in the side bar opens at the page you are reading. Its subject comes first, split into Lectures, Exercises, Tutorials and Appendix folds with a count on each; only the fold holding your page is open, and the other subjects sit as one row each. With every exercise listed it had grown to about two screens; open, it now fits in one.
  • Chapter numbers sit in their own column, so the combined APDR chapters 12a and 34a read as labels and the titles line up. Opening the list scrolls the side bar, not the page, to your place, and the site remembers which folds you leave open. The phone’s Contents sheet gets the same list.

2.40 APDR exercises reviewed

11 November 2026

  • Blackboard Exercise 2.1.3 now reads the nine-digit 1000000002 correctly as 256: it does not fit in a byte and equals 25610, while 111111112 equals FF16.
  • Blackboard Exercise 2 answers its “complete the table” parts with real tables, and shows the binary sums and differences column by column with their carries and borrows, the doubling steps for 0.1 and 0.625, and the steps from truth table to AB + AC + BC.
  • Blackboard Exercise 3 draws how word 14’s address splits into module and position under high- and low-order interleaving, and 3.3.2 states which address bits it interleaves on and what the other reading would give.
  • Computer Exercise 2 corrects two claims: at 1016 adding 1.0 lands exactly halfway between two floats (the tie keeps 1016), and Kahan summation recovers the stress case from n = 4, not 3. Computer Exercise 1’s code pieces now fit together: imports, the bytes-based helpers Task 10 calls, and what EXTENSION_TYPES and FORMAT_VALIDATORS hold.
  • Program names in the computer exercises are set as code, and the boilerplate after each answer is gone.
  • MEI Exercise 4.1a starts its induction at n = 1, since the sheet asks for n ∈ ℕ and this course’s ℕ begins at 1, and its hint now points to the induction step.

2.8 Freer labs

4 November 2026

  • Something new at the very bottom of the home page. Scroll all the way down.
  • Lab progress fix: an experiment whose challenges are all answered now counts on the home page even if you left or reloaded before pressing “Open the sandbox”. Opening the lab again restores any experiment that was missing. Ticking a guide skill no longer erases lab progress made in another tab.
  • EEI Chapter 1 guide: the professor fixed most of the slips we reported in the revised script, so “Slips in the notes” now lists only the three that remain (p. 47 notation, the swapped voltage names on pp. 84 and 90, and p. 92).
  • Bit patterns can be clicked: under every bit field in APDR 3 (encodings, unsigned and signed sums), APDR 4 (binary fractions, the IEEE exponent and fraction) and APDR 34a (binary sums, fraction decoding), a row of cells flips one bit per press and the result follows.
  • APDR 2 lab: type your own umbrella rule in C, W and R and see on which rows it differs from C′·(W+R); build any NAND-only circuit such as NAND(NAND(A, A), NAND(B, B)) and see its truth table, its name and how many gates it uses.
  • APDR 12a lab: type any function of A, B and C and pick any of the six variable orders to count its reduced OBDD nodes; set the two path delays in the hazard experiment and watch the gap open or close.
  • EEI Chapter 1 lab: save your charge layout and restore it on a later visit; the charge dropdowns use the site’s own list.
  • APDR labs show results as you type: the Convert, Decode, Calculate and Evaluate buttons are gone in APDR 2, 3, 4, 34a and 5, and each sandbox opens with its result already worked out.
  • APDR 12a lab: type both sides of your own identity in x and y and compare the rows; type minterms and don’t-cares or click Karnaugh-map cells (0 → 1 → d); type a truth table or click the f row to get its algebraic normal form.
  • Lab controls: dropdowns open the site’s own list instead of the browser’s, and every dropdown and typed field has the same box and width, so the controls line up.
  • Every lab experiment has an Explore freely button: open the sandbox at any time, then go back to the waiting challenge.
  • Lab fixes: the EEI point-charge sandbox opens with a charge instead of an empty field; sliders no longer stick out of the EEI controls; the duplicate slider strips on the MEI 2 and 3 stages are gone, which also uncovers the Pascal caption; labels sit next to their controls; tall APDR stages centre their drawing.
  • New challenge: after an experiment’s fixed challenges, thirty-five experiments generate fresh questions with computed answers, as many as you like: truth-table counts and set operations (MEI 1); binary values, distance intervals and binomial coefficients (MEI 2); mapping types, linear inverses, compositions and polar coordinates (MEI 3); base conversion, two’s-complement decoding, ASCII letters, binary addition and signed overflow (APDR 3); address bits, byte order and interleaving (APDR 5); square-root steps, the umbrella rule and NAND circuits (APDR 2); float spacing, binary fractions, multiplication, exponent bias and special values (APDR 4); identity checks, Karnaugh-map covers and algebraic normal forms (APDR 12a); integer encodings, binary sums, spacing, fractions and IEEE decoding (APDR 34a); plate-capacitor changes and series or parallel capacitors (EEI 1). Practice questions do not change your saved results.
  • APDR guides gain diagrams: flowcharts of an iteration, the stored-program machine and the three program checks; De Morgan Venn diagrams; gate-delay and static-1-hazard waveforms; Karnaugh maps with a group of four and a wrap-around group; 'A' in ASCII bits; the 4-bit two’s-complement wheel; floating-point gaps doubling; +0, +∞ and NaN bit patterns; a C pointer and two Python names for one list as memory boxes; a 16-bit address.
  • MEI 1 lab: type any logic formula in A, B, C for its truth table and verdict, or a set expression over your own X, A and B. MEI 2 lab: type any bits (up to 10), any centre and radius, any Pascal row up to 12.
  • MEI 3 lab: type your own rule in every experiment (mapping with your own sets, inverse test with a draggable horizontal line, composition of two rules, any graph) and drag the polar point.
  • MEI guides gain diagrams: implication and De Morgan as Venn diagrams, nested number systems, Pascal’s triangle, induction as a chain, the vertical-line test, f and f−1 mirrored in y = x, the discriminant’s three cases, and ex against ln x.
  • EEI Chapter 1 gains diagrams: like and unlike charges, adding fields at a point, dipole field lines, equipotentials, a Gaussian surface, a dielectric slab, potential against field, and the charging triangle.

2.7 Easier to follow

4 November 2026

  • Every study guide now reads on its own: each section opens with the idea in plain words before any symbols.
  • Formula cards that introduce notation say how to read them aloud, for example “the image of f is the set of all f(x) as x runs through X”.
  • In MEI Chapter 3, arrow diagrams show mappings, injective and surjective, inverses and composition, with the part that matters highlighted, on the page and in the PDF.
  • Each section has a near miss: an example that almost fits the definition and fails, with the reason.
  • Diagrams across the guides, on the page and in the PDF: Venn diagrams for sets and logic, number lines for intervals and bounds, bit-cell pictures for place values, two’s complement, IEEE 754, byte order and interleaving, the unit circle, field lines, and the memory hierarchy.
  • Formulas are spaced the way a book sets them (x ∈ A, (x, y), X × Y), and sub- and superscripts are larger.
