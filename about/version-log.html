2.8

Version Log

From Fundamentals to AI Apocalypse · newest first

What changed on the site and when. Chapters are added as the semester's lectures arrive; each version groups the changes released together.

2.8 Freer labs

4 November 2026

  • Lab controls: dropdowns open the site’s own list instead of the browser’s, and every dropdown and typed field has the same box and width, so the controls line up.
  • Every lab experiment has an Explore freely button: open the sandbox at any time, then go back to the waiting challenge.
  • Lab fixes: the EEI point-charge sandbox opens with a charge instead of an empty field; sliders no longer stick out of the EEI controls; the duplicate slider strips on the MEI 2 and 3 stages are gone, which also uncovers the Pascal caption; labels sit next to their controls; tall APDR stages centre their drawing.
  • New challenge: after an experiment’s fixed challenges, seventeen experiments generate fresh questions with computed answers, as many as you like: truth-table counts and set operations (MEI 1); binary values, distance intervals and binomial coefficients (MEI 2); mapping types, linear inverses, compositions and polar coordinates (MEI 3); base conversion, two’s-complement decoding, ASCII letters, binary addition and signed overflow (APDR 3); address bits, byte order and interleaving (APDR 5). Practice questions do not change your saved results.
  • APDR guides gain diagrams: flowcharts of an iteration, the stored-program machine and the three program checks; De Morgan Venn diagrams; gate-delay and static-1-hazard waveforms; Karnaugh maps with a group of four and a wrap-around group; 'A' in ASCII bits; the 4-bit two’s-complement wheel; floating-point gaps doubling; +0, +∞ and NaN bit patterns; a C pointer and two Python names for one list as memory boxes; a 16-bit address.
  • MEI 1 lab: type any logic formula in A, B, C for its truth table and verdict, or a set expression over your own X, A and B. MEI 2 lab: type any bits (up to 10), any centre and radius, any Pascal row up to 12.
  • MEI 3 lab: type your own rule in every experiment (mapping with your own sets, inverse test with a draggable horizontal line, composition of two rules, any graph) and drag the polar point.
  • MEI guides gain diagrams: implication and De Morgan as Venn diagrams, nested number systems, Pascal’s triangle, induction as a chain, the vertical-line test, f and f−1 mirrored in y = x, the discriminant’s three cases, and ex against ln x.
  • EEI Chapter 1 gains diagrams: like and unlike charges, adding fields at a point, dipole field lines, equipotentials, a Gaussian surface, a dielectric slab, potential against field, and the charging triangle.

2.7 Easier to follow

4 November 2026

  • Every study guide now reads on its own: each section opens with the idea in plain words before any symbols.
  • Formula cards that introduce notation say how to read them aloud, for example “the image of f is the set of all f(x) as x runs through X”.
  • In MEI Chapter 3, arrow diagrams show mappings, injective and surjective, inverses and composition, with the part that matters highlighted, on the page and in the PDF.
  • Each section has a near miss: an example that almost fits the definition and fails, with the reason.
  • Diagrams across the guides, on the page and in the PDF: Venn diagrams for sets and logic, number lines for intervals and bounds, bit-cell pictures for place values, two’s complement, IEEE 754, byte order and interleaving, the unit circle, field lines, and the memory hierarchy.
  • Formulas are spaced the way a book sets them (x ∈ A, (x, y), X × Y), and sub- and superscripts are larger.

2.6 HUD layer

4 November 2026

  • A radar in the hub header shows each chapter you have started as a contact; the sweep passes over them.
  • Headings decode into place the first time they come into view, sweeping across the line.
  • A reading chip at the top of the page shows the section you are in, how far through it you are and the minutes left.
  • Formula cards, worked examples and recall checks have corner brackets; signal lines have a soft glow.
  • The reading column is wider, headings that nearly fit one line are fitted to it, and every line in a section ends at the same edge.
  • Formula cards never scroll sideways: long formulas break before a relation sign instead. A symmetric settings gear, and no sideways scrolling on phones.
  • The contents rail folds away to a slim strip on wide screens, and the site remembers it.
  • Reading columns, cards and chapter buttons sit centred, with equal space on each side.
  • New pages: How to use, and this version log.

2.5 Settings and folding

4 November 2026

  • Settings: theme (System, Light, Dark), motion (System, Reduced) and text size (Default, Large).
  • A ring marks the furthest chapter you have reached, and a signal travels down the route ahead.
  • The hub's subjects and the not-yet-due board rows fold, and remember how you left them.
  • APDR and EEI formula cards use the same notation as the maths guides.

2.4 Motion and navigation

3 November 2026

  • Page changes sweep in, and a reading-progress bar runs along the top of each guide.
  • The hub route lights up as you start chapters, and skill bars fill as you tick.
  • Worked examples can be stepped through, symbols trace through a section on hover, and the concept map shows direction.
  • The contents rail folds, follows your reading position, and lists every chapter; a phone gets a Contents bar.
  • Lab challenges animate step by step and answer buttons respond when pressed.

2.3 MEI and APDR Chapter 5

  • MEI Chapter 1 lab: tautology, unique existence, sensor vote and interval experiments.

  • Sum, product, integral, union and intersection limits are stacked properly on the site and in the PDFs.
  • Better phone layout and dark-mode lab controls; every page and PDF credits the author.
