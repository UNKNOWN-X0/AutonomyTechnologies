Real Numbers Lab

Mathematics for Engineers I · Module 62765 · Chapter 2

Commit to a prediction, rate your confidence, then reveal the mechanism. Finish an experiment’s four challenges to open its controls and try new values.

How the lab works

  1. Predict. Choose an answer before the stage changes.
  2. Rate your confidence. A sure but wrong answer marks an idea to revisit first.
  3. Reveal. Watch the rule play out and follow its link back to the guide.
  4. Explore. Finish all four challenges to open that experiment’s controls.

2.1 Number systems: binary place value

Each bit marks a power of two. Read the places from right to left, then add the marked values to recover the integer.

After challenges, drag the stage marker or focus it and use arrow keys.

Choose a binary numeral

2.2 Suprema: rationals around √2

Rational lower and upper bounds can squeeze toward √2. The real numbers contain the least upper bound even though this particular set contains no maximum.

After challenges, drag or focus the marker to change decimal precision.

Set the decimal precision

2.3 Absolute value: distance on the line

The inequality |x−a|≤r describes all points at distance at most r from a. The center shifts the interval; the radius sets its width.

After challenges, drag or focus the stage marker; the radius stays adjustable below.

Choose a center and radius

2.4 Binomial coefficients: Pascal’s rows

Each interior coefficient is the sum of the two above it. Row n supplies the coefficients for (a+b)n, while the term exponents run in opposite directions.

After challenges, choose a row to inspect its coefficients.

Choose a Pascal row

2.5 Induction: start and advance

An induction proof establishes a starting case, then shows that any allowed case passes the statement to the next one. The chain cannot move until its first link is true.

After challenges, check the sum formula at a chosen n.

Choose an induction index
