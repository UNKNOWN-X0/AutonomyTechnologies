Statements, Logic, and Sets Lab

Mathematics for Engineers I · Module 62765 · Chapter 1

Commit to a prediction, rate your confidence, then reveal the rule in a small, concrete case. When the challenges are complete, use the controls to test new values.

How the lab works

  1. Predict. Choose an answer before the stage changes.
  2. Rate your confidence. A sure but wrong answer marks an idea to revisit first.
  3. Reveal. Compare the result and follow its link back to the guide.
  4. Explore. Finish all four challenges to open each experiment’s controls.

1.1 Truth tables: implication and equivalence

Compare truth values row by row. Swapping the sides of an implication changes its meaning; the contrapositive is the form that stays equivalent.

After challenges: drag the marker or focus it and use arrow keys.

1.1 Quantifiers: domain and counterexample

A quantified claim is evaluated over its stated domain. One counterexample is enough to disprove a universal claim.

1.2 Set operations: membership and De Morgan

Set operations can be checked element by element: membership in a union is OR, while membership in an intersection is AND.

Build every subset of a small set and preserve coordinate order in Cartesian products. These structures are easy to confuse when their elements are written compactly.
