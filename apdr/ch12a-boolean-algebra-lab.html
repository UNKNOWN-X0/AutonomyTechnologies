APDR

Boolean Algebra and Minimisation Lab

Algorithms, Programming, and Data Representation · Module 92313 · Chapter 12a

Predict first, then reveal. Check Boolean identities, build functions from NAND, minimise with maps, compare representations, and prevent a circuit glitch.

How the lab works

  1. Predict. Commit to an answer before the stage moves.
  2. Rate your confidence. A sure but wrong answer is useful; it appears under Your results.
  3. Watch the reveal. Follow the truth rows, gate construction, grouping, or signal handoff, then review the linked guide section.
  4. Explore. Finish an experiment to open its controls and tick its lab skill.

1.2 Identity checks: compare every row

Check an identity by evaluating both sides on every input row. A law is not established by a few examples, but a counterexample is enough to disprove it.

Equal outputs on every row mean the expressions are equivalent

1.4 Karnaugh maps: group adjacent ones

1.5 Shannon expansion: split on an input

Setting a variable to 0 and 1 gives two cofactors. A fixed variable order turns these splits into an OBDD; reduction removes duplicate tests and subgraphs.
